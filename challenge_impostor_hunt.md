# Activity 2: AI Cheating and Impostor Hunt

Can you tell which answer was written by a student and which was written mostly by AI? In this activity, you will prepare a pair of answers, try to fool your classmates, and serve as a judge for their submissions.

## 1. Choose completed homework

Choose **one homework assignment you have already finished**. It must require a short essay or longer written answers. Multiple-choice and fill-in-the-blank assignments do not qualify.

Include the original question(s) and enough assignment context for a judge to understand what the answers should address. Both versions must answer the same question(s).

## 2. Prepare two answers

**Each answer must contain at least 150 words**, excluding the question, headings, and references. Choose completed homework with enough written material to meet this minimum so judges have enough writing to compare.

| Version | Requirements |
|---|---|
| **Your own answer** | Use your genuinely completed work. AI assistance must be **less than 20%** and limited to grammar correction or sentence flow. For example, write the entire answer yourself first, then use AI only for light editing. The ideas, reasoning, and substantive writing must be yours. |
| **The impostor** | Create an answer that is entirely AI-generated or **more than 80% AI-generated**. You may refine your prompts and choose among AI drafts to make this version convincing, while keeping it above the 80% threshold. |

These percentages describe the approximate contribution of AI to creating the answer. Report your process honestly in the private answer key.

**Try your best to confuse the judges.** Make both versions plausible answers to the homework. You may ask AI to use a style similar to your own, but your own answer must still meet the less-than-20% rule.

Label the versions **Answer A** and **Answer B**, choosing which comes first. Use the same presentation format for both, and leave authorship labels and identifying notes out of the versions shown to judges. Keep the answer key secret until voting is finished.

## 3. Submit in Canvas

Submit the following by the deadline posted in Canvas:

1. The homework question(s), with any instructions or context needed to understand them.
2. Answer A, at least 150 words.
3. Answer B, at least 150 words.
4. A **private answer key for the instructor** identifying which answer is your own and which is the impostor, with a brief description of how AI was used for each.

Only the question(s) and the A/B answers should be shown to judges. Submit the answer key through an instructor-only Canvas submission so classmates cannot see it before voting.

## 4. Judge in class

Every student is both an author and a judge.

1. Read each classmate's question(s) and both answers.
2. Vote for **exactly one answer, A or B, as the impostor**.
3. Submit one vote per pair. Do not vote on your own submission.
4. Wait until votes are recorded before revealing the answer key.

## 5. Earn points

Each judge's vote awards **one judging point or one bonus point**:

| Vote result | Who earns the point? |
|---|---|
| The judge correctly identifies the AI-generated impostor. | The **judge** earns 1 judging point. |
| The judge chooses the student's own answer and fails to catch the impostor. | The **author of the pair** earns 1 bonus point. |

A wrong vote earns the author one bonus point for that judge. It does not count as two separate mistakes. Keep judging points and bonus points separate, then add them for your total game score.

## 6. Passing requirement

**To pass this activity, your judging points plus bonus points must reach at least half of the possible judging points. A total below that threshold fails the activity.** Round the required score up to the next whole point when necessary.

The passing threshold is based **only on the available judging points**. Fooling classmates earns bonus points, which are added to your score without increasing the passing threshold. With **N participating students**, if everyone judges every other student's pair:

- You can earn up to **N - 1** points by judging correctly.
- Your passing score is **(N - 1) / 2, rounded up**.
- Each classmate you fool adds **1 bonus point** to your judging score.
- Bonus points can help you reach the passing score or raise your total above the judging maximum.

For example, with **12 students**, you can judge 11 pairs, so there are **11 possible judging points**. You need **6 total points** to pass. Six correct judgments would pass without a bonus; five correct judgments plus one classmate fooled would also pass.

The game score is separate from the course's 5-point Activity 2 allocation. Follow Canvas for course grading and the separately scheduled reflection.
